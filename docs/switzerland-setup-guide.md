# Turnstile — Switzerland Setup Guide

*2026-10-08*

How to incorporate and license Turnstile as a Swiss crypto company, and the path to relocating.

## Step 1: Form a GmbH in Zug (~2–3 weeks, ~CHF 5K–15K)

- **GmbH** (not AG) — minimum share capital is CHF 20,000
- **Canton of Zug** — "Crypto Valley", lowest corporate tax in Switzerland, crypto-friendly commercial register
- **You stay in Israel** — fully legal. You own 100% of the company. No Swiss residency or citizenship needed
- **Nominee director** — you must have one Swiss-resident person with signing authority. Firms like SwissFirma or Goldblum provide this for CHF 2K–5K/year. You're registered as co-director

**Formation costs:**

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Formation (notary, register) | CHF 750–5,000 (DIY vs full-service package) |
| Share capital (deposited, yours) | CHF 20,000 |
| Nominee director + registered office | CHF 2,000–5,000/year |
| Bank account opening | CHF 0–500 |

## Step 2: Open a Swiss bank account (~2–4 weeks)

Options:
- **Crypto-friendly banks**: Sygnum, SEBA, Bank Frick (Liechtenstein, works with Swiss companies)
- **Traditional banks**: possible but they'll ask questions about the crypto model. Having the SRO membership helps
- The CHF 20,000 share capital gets deposited here

## Step 3: Join VQF (SRO membership) (~8–12 weeks, CHF 4K–6K)

VQF (Verein zur Qualitätssicherung von Finanzdienstleistungen) in Zug is the main SRO for crypto companies. Membership makes you a regulated financial intermediary under AMLA (Anti-Money Laundering Act).

**What you need to submit:**

1. AML/KYC compliance framework (policies and procedures)
2. Business plan describing Turnstile's model
3. Description of risk management
4. Background checks on founders/directors
5. Proof of Swiss office and director

**What you get:**

- Legally authorized to operate as a financial intermediary
- FINMA-recognized compliance status
- Banks take you more seriously
- Grandfathered into the upcoming FINMA Crypto Institution transition (expected late 2026/2027)

## Step 4: Set up AML/KYC compliance

Required before VQF will approve you:
- Know Your Customer procedures for your clients (API providers)
- Transaction monitoring (you already have the ledger)
- Suspicious activity reporting process
- Record keeping (5+ years)
- You can hire a Swiss compliance consultant or use a compliance-as-a-service provider

## Step 5: Start operating

Once VQF membership is approved, you can legally:
- Receive USDC payments from AI agents
- Hold crypto on behalf of clients (within SRO scope)
- Convert and pay out fiat to clients

## Timeline

| Step | Duration |
|---|---|
| GmbH formation | 2–3 weeks |
| Bank account | 2–4 weeks (parallel with formation) |
| AML/KYC framework | 2–4 weeks (parallel) |
| VQF application + approval | 8–12 weeks |
| **Total** | **~3–4 months** |

## Total first-year cost

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| GmbH formation (full service) | CHF 5,000–15,000 |
| Share capital (your money, stays in company) | CHF 20,000 |
| VQF membership first year | CHF 4,000–6,000 |
| Nominee director + office | CHF 3,000–5,000 |
| AML compliance setup | CHF 3,000–8,000 |
| Accounting / annual audit | CHF 3,000–5,000 |
| **Total (excl. share capital)** | **CHF 18,000–39,000 (~$20K–$44K)** |

## Timing window

FINMA proposed new "Crypto Institution" and "Payment Institution" license categories in October 2025. These are expected to take effect late 2026 or 2027. Companies that complete SRO membership now get grandfathered into a transition regime rather than starting from scratch under the new rules.

## Relocating to Switzerland

### The route: Self-Employment B Permit

Israel is non-EU/EFTA, so there's no free movement. You'd apply under Article 19 FNIA for a self-employment residence permit.

**What you need to show:**

- A Swiss GmbH that's already registered and operating
- Business plan proving the company benefits the Swiss economy (jobs, tax revenue, innovation)
- Financial projections
- Proof you have sufficient funds
- Adequate housing in Switzerland
- Skills/expertise not readily available locally

**Process:**

1. Canton of Zug reviews your application
2. If approved locally, it goes to federal level (SEM — State Secretariat for Migration)
3. Subject to annual quotas — 4,500 B permits total for all non-EU nationals across all of Switzerland in 2026
4. If granted, B permit = 5 years residency, renewable

### Realistic assessment

**In your favor:**

- Zug is crypto-friendly and actively courts fintech companies
- A licensed, operational crypto company with revenue is a real business
- You're creating a Swiss entity, paying Swiss taxes, potentially hiring

**Against you:**

- No startup visa — Switzerland has nothing like the UAE golden visa or Estonia e-Residency
- Quota system is tight — 4,500 B permits shared across the entire country for all non-EU nationals
- Self-employment permits for non-EU founders have a low approval rate
- You need to demonstrate skills that Swiss/EU candidates can't provide
- Cost of living in Zug is very high (~CHF 3,000–4,000/month for a modest apartment)

### Alternative approaches

| Approach | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Stay in Israel, nominee director** | Cheapest. Run the company remotely. Visit Switzerland for meetings on tourist entry (90 days/180 days visa-free for Israelis). Most crypto founders in this position do this. |
| **Move later** | Get the company running, generate revenue, hire 1–2 Swiss employees. Then apply for the B permit with a stronger case. |
| **Intra-company transfer** | If Turnstile grows, set up an Israeli entity first, then "transfer" yourself to the Swiss branch. Slightly different permit path. |
| **Lump-sum taxation** | If you have significant capital (~CHF 500K+ annual living expenses), some cantons offer residency based on lump-sum tax agreements. Not Zug — they abolished this. |

### Recommendation

Don't make the move a prerequisite for starting the company. Form the GmbH, get VQF membership, start operating — all from Israel with a nominee director. If the business takes off in 6–12 months, you'll have a much stronger permit application with an operational, revenue-generating, regulated Swiss company.
